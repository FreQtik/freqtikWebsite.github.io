5-Minute Quickstart

Make one IR, hear what it does, and the rest of the plugin becomes easier to place.

1. Put Anvil on a track

Use a sound you know well: guitar, piano, synth, drums or voice. A fairly dry source makes the effect easiest to judge.

Keep the listening level sensible while experimenting. Unusual IRs can create strong resonances.

2. Load something into A

Click Load in A, or drag a compatible audio file onto the A panel.

Try a factory IR, one of your own IRs, or a recording.

Click the A panel to hear A.

Want a fast starting point?

Press the small Random button beside A's Load control. It can choose another available IR and give the slot a new treatment without randomly boosting Gain.

3. Change one thing

Start with:

  • Time — shorter or longer
  • Color — add a changed copy back into the IR
  • Texture — let that Color idea build into more layers

Move one control and listen to what changes.

4. Add B

Load something different into B.

Click the Morph panel and move Morph.

Start with Time Morph, then compare another Morph mode.

5. Add movement

Enable A→B Lerp.

Use:

  • Lerp Start to choose when the movement begins
  • Lerp Time to choose how long the movement takes

Both are shown in milliseconds.

For a custom transition, choose Draw and paint the A→B movement directly.

Curves, Draw, Path, Glue & Omni

6. Keep only the useful part

In EDIT, move Start and End around the part you want.

  • Start before End = forward
  • Start after End = reverse
  • link them = scan one fixed-size window through the IR

Need tiny movements?

  • Shift + drag = fine
  • Ctrl + drag = very fine

7. Bake it

When you hear something worth keeping, press Bake.

The exported WAV can be used in another compatible convolution plugin, loaded back into Anvil, or combined with another result.

Good first experiment

Record a short metal hit, clap, scrape or object impact. Find an interesting region in EDIT, Bake it, then load that Bake back into Anvil and Morph it with a completely different IR.
