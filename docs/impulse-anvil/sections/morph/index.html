Morph & A→B Lerp

Morph decides what relationship should exist between A and B.

Sometimes that means a direct blend. Sometimes the useful result is what the sources share, what only one source contains, or a new acoustic relationship built from both.

Three families in the current full build

The Morph browser groups the 21 modes into a core set plus two named families. The browser itself explains Use it for, How Morph behaves and Technical for the selected mode.

Core Morph

  • Time Morph - direct sample-by-sample movement between prepared A and B.
  • Spectral - move the frequency-magnitude structure of A toward B with a stable phase relationship.
  • Spectral BandSwap - alternate broad spectral bands between A and B and swap their ownership through Morph.
  • Spectral ZigZag - use denser alternating spectral stripes for more fractured hybrids.
  • Stereo Slot Swap - send the A/B relationship in opposite directions across left and right.
  • Mid/Side Boundary - keep A as the centre/mid anchor while B increasingly defines the outside/side field.

Residual / Decompose

  • Raw Difference - literal directional subtraction; timing and level mismatches remain part of the result.
  • Aligned Difference - align the main events first, then subtract.
  • Matched Residual - align and broadband level-match before subtraction.
  • Spectral Carve - use bounded frequency-dependent subtraction instead of removing the same amount everywhere.
  • Similarity Residual - remove the reference more strongly where A and B appear genuinely similar.
  • Common - estimate the component shared by A and B.
  • Unique A - keep what remains characteristic of A after estimated shared material is removed.
  • Unique B - keep what remains characteristic of B after estimated shared material is removed.
  • Difference Focus - emphasize regions where the two responses disagree most.
  • Spectro-Temporal Residual - vary similarity and subtraction locally across both time and frequency.

Acoustic Interaction

  • Nest - cascade A and B as acoustic transfer functions, effectively placing one response through the other.
  • Transfer - build a stabilized A→B or B→A transfer operator.
  • Ghost - combine the magnitude/body of one response with the phase structure of the other.
  • Eclipse - search for a stable deep-cancellation relationship and keep the surviving residual.
  • Spectral Time Shear - create a time-frequency hybrid where low, mid and high regions follow different A/B trajectories.

Direction matters in some modes

Residual and Acoustic Interaction modes can expose a small direction control because the question changes when A is the base instead of B. Unique A and Unique B already encode their direction in the mode name.

Morph amount can become an authored timeline

Built-in curves, Draw, Path and Glue Path can control how the selected relationship develops through the response. In residual modes, vertical depth means moving from the selected base toward the residual/common/unique target.

A→B Lerp

A→B Lerp builds one static IR whose internal relationship changes over time. It is not simply a DAW automation recording. The selected Morph mode and curve/path are part of the response construction, and Bake can commit that construction to WAV.

Glue Path is different. Glue owns its constructed duration from the strokes you paint and ignores Lerp Start/Time.
