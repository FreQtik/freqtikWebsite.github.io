Morph & A→B Lerp

Morph is where A and B become one new impulse response.

The Morph control sets the A/B balance. The selected Morph mode decides how the two sources are combined.

The six Morph modes

Time Morph

The most direct blend. A and B meet through their actual time-domain shapes.

Useful for: clear transitions, natural crossfades and an easy reference point before trying the other modes.

Spectral

Combines more of the tonal/frequency fingerprint of A and B.

Useful for: fused tones where the result feels less like two IRs simply mixed together.

Spectral BandSwap

Different frequency areas can lean toward different sources.

Useful for: hybrids where one part of the spectrum carries more A while another carries more B.

Spectral ZigZag

Creates a more divided, artificial spectral relationship.

Useful for: sharper synthetic colors, unstable tones and more obviously transformed material.

Stereo Slot Swap

The left and right sides travel through A and B in opposite directions.

Useful for: asymmetrical stereo fields and wide A/B relationships.

Mid/Side Boundary

A forms more of the center foundation while B enters the side field.

Useful for: keeping a strong middle while building a different texture around it.

Morph Random

The small Random button in Morph tries another A/B relationship without replacing the A or B files.

It can change the Morph position, Morph mode, Curve/K, Align and Lerp state.

Align

Align moves the dominant peaks of A and B toward a shared start before they are combined.

This can make attacks meet more cleanly.

Align can also remove material that existed before the moved peak. If an IR has a useful lead-in, swell, scrape or early texture before its main hit, compare Align on and off.

  • Align on: tighter meeting point
  • Align off: keep the original start timing and lead-in

A→B Lerp

Normal Morph gives you one fixed A/B position.

A→B Lerp builds movement inside the IR itself:

BEGINNING                       END
more A  →  changing blend  →  more B

The selected Morph mode is used during that movement.

Lerp Start

Sets the exact time where the transition begins.

0 ms starts at the beginning of the IR.

Lerp Time

Sets how long the transition takes.

0 ms creates an instant switch at Lerp Start.

The available Start/Time range follows the current prepared A/B length. If Time, Color or Texture makes the endpoints longer, the Lerp timing range follows that growth.

Use the A and B waveforms as a map

The curve display shows the A and B waveforms behind the transition area.

This makes Draw and Path easier to place by eye.

You can recognize:

  • a strong transient;
  • a quiet gap;
  • a dense section;
  • a tail;
  • a later reflection or event;

and draw the transition or Path around those visible landmarks.

If A and B have different lengths, the waveforms also make it obvious where one source has already ended.

Curve shapes

Choose Linear, Exp, Log, S-Curve, Draw or Path.

The built-in curves shape the A→B movement automatically.

Draw lets you paint that movement yourself.

Path also lets you control which point in source-time is read, including moving backward through earlier material.

Bake

A→B Lerp uses the same visible Bake button as the rest of Anvil.

Preview the result, then Bake when you want to keep it.

Technical background

The longer prepared A/B endpoint defines the main Lerp source domain. Absolute Lerp Start and Time are clamped to the current available endpoint duration. Time Morph can run as a continuous time-varying blend; the spectral/spatial modes use mode-aware rendering so Preview and Bake share the same prepared result.
