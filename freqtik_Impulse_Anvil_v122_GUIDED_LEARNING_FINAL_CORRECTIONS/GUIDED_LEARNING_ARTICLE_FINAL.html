Learn Impulse Anvil by Doing

This is not a page you have to study. It is a course you can work through inside the plugin.

Most cards ask you to do one thing, listen to what changed, then move on. The next card unlocks when you press Done.

Full-workstation course: this path eventually uses Texture Depth, Color 2, Bake, the full Morph collection and A→B Lerp. If you are using the free demo, start with the 5-Minute Quickstart.
BEFORE YOU START

You don't need to know what an IR is

In Impulse Anvil, any WAV can become an IR.

A kick drum, hi-hat, shaker, clap, noise, a bad recording artifact — whatever. You can also use AIF, AIFF and FLAC.

If your DAW has a file browser, you can drag a sound straight onto A or B. You can also record your own material: scratch a surface, record water, room noise, a strange object, or anything else that gives you an interesting shape.

For the first lessons, use material around one second long. It is long enough to hear what you are doing and short enough that changes stay easy to follow.

Your job is not to make every step sound good. Make the differences obvious first. Once you know what a control does, you can use it subtly.

CHAPTER 1

Meet A

Learn one slot before you think about two. By the end of this chapter, A should already feel like an instrument.

A01Ready

Load your first IR

Learn how material gets into Anvil.

  1. Click the A panel once to select it.
  2. Click the current filename in A. This opens the IR Browser.
  3. Pick any file you like.
  4. Double-click the file to load it instantly.

Notice: your track changes because A now has something shaping it.

Alternative: drag a WAV, AIF, AIFF or FLAC directly onto A.

A02Locked

Before you go wild: Undo

Know that experimenting is reversible.

  1. Move Time far enough that you clearly hear a change.
  2. Press Undo.
  3. Press Redo.

Remember: if something sounded amazing two seconds ago and you think “OH NO, I broke it,” use Undo.

A03Locked

Turn Time

Hear how much one IR can change before you add anything else.

  1. Turn Time down and listen.
  2. Turn it up and listen again.
  3. Move between both extremes a few times.

Notice: you are still using the same file, but the underlying IR can change dramatically.

A04Locked

Normalize and Gain

Learn automatic loudness first, then manual loudness.

If the IR is very quiet, turn on Norm in A. Normalize automatically brings that slot up for you.

Then use Gain to set the level where you actually want it.

Notice: A Gain still works after A Normalize.

A05Locked

Add your first Color

Create a changed copy of A and hear it immediately.

  1. Turn Color 1 · Time. Moving a Color knob turns that Color on automatically.
  2. Make the Time change obvious.
  3. Move it back and forth while your track plays.

Listen: Color Time changes the added Color layer without replacing your original A.

A06Locked

Control how much Color you hear

Meet Amount.

Move Color 1 · Amount down, then up.

Listen: Amount controls how loud that added Color layer is. Use it to make the change subtle or let it take over.

A07Locked

Push Offset all the way out

Make delay/spacing impossible to miss.

  1. Set Color 1 · Offset close to 500 ms.
  2. Listen.
  3. Now make Offset very small.
  4. Go back and forth between tiny and huge values.

Notice: small Offset values overlap closely with the original. Large values separate the Color farther in time.

A08Locked

Build a texture

See what Texture Depth does instead of reading a definition.

  1. Set Texture Depth to 2.
  2. Look at the waveform in EDIT.
  3. Move Color 1 Offset and watch/listen to how the repeated material moves.
  4. Set Texture Depth to 4.
  5. Try a very small Offset and then a very large one.

Notice: compare Depth 2 with Depth 4. Don't memorize it — hear and see the difference.

A09Locked

Add Color 2

Hear how two Colors change the texture.

  1. Move Color 2 · Time to turn Color 2 on.
  2. Change Color 2 Amount and Offset.
  3. Move Texture Depth again.
  4. Change Color 1, then Color 2, and listen to how the texture reacts.

Notice: with two Colors active, Texture has two different Color layers to work with instead of reusing only one.

What happens underneath?

With one Color active, Texture reuses that Color. With both active, the extra layers alternate Color 1 / Color 2.

A10Locked

Reset Color — then bring it back

Learn the reset gesture without throwing away the texture you just built.

  1. Double-click the COLOR title in A.
  2. Notice: the slot's Color/Texture setup resets while the IR stays loaded.
  3. Now press Undo to bring your Color/Texture work back.

Keep it: Chapter 2 will use the complexity you just built.

CHAPTER 2

Shape the result

You made complexity. Now learn how to remove what you don't want, keep the part you do want, and finish the IR.

S01Locked

Move an EQ Bell

Layering can create frequencies you don't like. That's why EQ is here.

  1. Open EQ.
  2. Drag the selected Bell point left/right and up/down.
  3. Use the mouse wheel over the graph to make that Bell wider or narrower.

Tip: double-click another Bell point to select that Bell.

Listen: move it until something annoying disappears or something interesting appears.

S02Locked

Cut lows and highs

Meet Highpass and Lowpass by using them.

Move HP up until you clearly hear low frequencies disappear.

Move LP down until you clearly hear high frequencies disappear.

Then put both somewhere that makes the sound more useful or more interesting.

S03Locked

Keep only the part you like

This is what EDIT is for.

Imagine you like your current IR, but only one spot of it.

  1. Open EDIT.
  2. Move Start.
  3. Move End.
  4. Keep moving them while the track plays until you isolate something interesting.

Notice: you are surgically choosing which part of the current IR is allowed to remain.

S04Locked

Turn Start + End into a search window

Scan through the IR without changing the size of your selection.

  1. Put Start and End around a short piece.
  2. Click the tiny link between Start and End.
  3. Move Start or End and scan that fixed-size window through the IR.
  4. Start a new drag while holding Shift for fine movement.
  5. Start a new drag while holding Ctrl for very fine movement.

Use this when: you know there is one good little spot somewhere, but you don't know exactly where yet.

S05Locked

Reverse it, then shape the edges

Learn three creative EDIT tricks at once because they belong together.

  1. Move End before Start. The selected IR plays backwards.
  2. Try Fade In.
  3. Try a normal Fade Out.
  4. Turn on Fade Out +.
  5. Change the Fade Out length again.

Notice: Fade Out + flips the ending behavior so the end can swell upward instead of simply fading away.

S06Locked

Meet OUT Normalize and Limiter

Understand the two switches that are on by default.

OUT Normalize manages the loudness of the final IR automatically.

This is different from Norm in A or B: A/B Normalize belongs to that one slot. OUT Normalize happens to the final prepared IR, whether you are hearing A, B or Morph.

Limiter is also on by default and catches peaks that become too strong.

S07Locked

Take manual control with IR In

Hear why IR In can seem less useful while OUT Normalize is on.

  1. Move IR In while OUT Normalize is on.
  2. Turn OUT Normalize off.
  3. Move IR In again.

Notice: with Normalize off, IR In becomes your direct control over the prepared/baked IR level.

S08Locked

Width — then the listening controls

Separate what changes the IR from what only changes how you hear it in the plugin.

Width = 0 makes the prepared IR mono. Width = 1 keeps its original width. Go above 1 to widen the stereo information that is already there.

Now look at Wet Level, Dry/Wet and Out. These are for how you listen inside the plugin.

At 100% Dry/Wet, you are hearing only the wet result, so Wet Level becomes the main playback level for that processed sound.

Important: Bake keeps the IR you've built. Dry/Wet, Wet Level and Out are only for how you listen to it inside the plugin, so those three aren't baked.
S09Locked

Bake your first IR

Turn what you hear into a file you can keep.

  1. Make sure A is the active panel.
  2. Press BAKE.
  3. Name the file something obvious like My first IR.
  4. Save it.

Congratulations: you just created your first own IR.

CHAPTER 3

Build B yourself

Everything you learned inside A also exists in B. This time the course gives you less help on purpose.

B01Locked

Make a B you like

Try to remember the A workflow instead of reading it again.

Load a different IR into B and shape B until you like it.

Use B's Time, Normalize/Gain, Colors and Texture if you want them.

For now: don't change EQ, EDIT or OUT. Try to build B from the slot controls alone.

If you forget something, scroll back. That's part of learning it.

B02Locked

Notice what A and B share

Understand where EQ, EDIT and OUT sit in the workflow.

Click A, then B, and listen.

Notice: the same EQ, EDIT and OUT settings affect whichever prepared result you are hearing. They are not a second private set of controls for B.

Double-click the EQ, EDIT or OUT title to reset that section to default.

B03Locked

Reset everything except your files

Know the clean-start button before Morph gets involved.

Open OPTIONS and find:

Init / Reset Controls (keep IRs)

You don't have to use it right now. Just know where it is: it resets the controls while keeping your chosen A and B files in place.

CHAPTER 4

Meet Morph

You built A. You built B. Now let Anvil create relationships between them.

Don't see this as something hard. There is no correct Morph sound. There is what you hear, what inspires you, and what you want to keep.
M01Locked

Make one static Morph

Learn what Morph does before movement is involved.

  1. Click Morph.
  2. Choose Time Morph.
  3. Turn the Morph knob from A toward B.

The more you move toward A, the more the result favors A. Move toward B and it favors B.

If one side overwhelms the other, go back to A/B and adjust their Gain until the transition feels more even.

Right now the IR is static. The Morph knob chooses one position and Anvil builds that one result.
M02Locked

Change the curve, then turn Morph again

Hear that the same knob can travel between A and B differently.

Try Linear, Exp, Log and S-Curve. Move the Morph knob after each change.

Notice: the knob is still choosing one static Morph position, but the curve changes how that position is shaped between A and B.

Save Draw, Path, Glue and Omni for a little later.

M03Locked

Taste every Morph

No studying allowed.

The Morph collection can look scary. Treat it like a box of candy for five minutes.

  1. Keep the same A and B.
  2. Go through the Morph modes one by one.
  3. Move Morph for each one.
  4. Just listen.

Some may sound similar. Some may sound broken in a useful way. Some may immediately give you an idea.

You don't need to understand them yet. You only need to have heard them once.

When you want the meaning behind a mode, use the human-first Morph cards →

CHAPTER 5 · THE BIG ONE

Write movement into the IR

This is where static Morph becomes A→B Lerp.

Static Morph = one position. A→B Lerp = movement inside the IR.
L01Locked

Turn on A→B Lerp

This is the switch that makes the movement part of the IR.

Imagine the Morph knob moving by itself, and that whole movement being written into one IR.

That's A→B Lerp.

  1. Choose Time Morph.
  2. Choose a simple Linear curve.
  3. Turn on A→B Lerp.
Without A→B Lerp, Morph gives you one static position.
With A→B Lerp, the movement itself becomes part of the IR.

You don't have to automate Morph later. Bake can keep that movement inside the file.

L02Locked

Choose where the movement starts — and how long it takes

Meet Lerp Start and Lerp Time only after you know why Lerp exists.

Move Lerp Start. This decides where the A→B movement begins inside the IR.

Move Lerp Time. This decides how long the movement takes.

Make Lerp Time short and the change happens faster. Make it long and the movement spreads across more of the IR.

Make the difference obvious first. Fine-tune later.

L03Locked

Draw the movement yourself

Stop choosing a preset curve and paint what A→B Lerp should do.

  1. Keep A→B Lerp on.
  2. Choose Draw.
  3. Paint an obvious movement from A toward B and back.
  4. Listen.

Notice: the curve is now your movement instead of a predefined shape.

The tiny controls around the curve are there to make editing easier. Hover them if you forget what one does.

L04Locked

Use Path to go backward in the source

Learn that the route can choose which point in time is read.

  1. Choose Path.
  2. Draw forward.
  3. Move backward in X.
  4. Continue forward again.

Listen: moving backward in X can revisit an earlier part of the source instead of forcing time to move only forward.

L05Locked

Glue separate pieces together

Build a response from several strokes instead of one continuous trip.

  1. Choose Glue Path.
  2. Paint one stroke over something interesting.
  3. Paint a second stroke somewhere else.
  4. Listen to the result as one new sequence.

Notice: Glue builds its own sequence from your strokes. Its timing comes from horizontal source-time travel rather than Lerp Start/Time.

L06Locked

Make the gesture itself create time with Omni

Hear why Omni is not just another Path.

  1. Choose Omni Path.
  2. Paint a mostly horizontal stroke.
  3. Paint a steep stroke with a lot of vertical travel.
  4. Compare them.

Notice: in Omni, horizontal and vertical travel consume output time. X still chooses source-time. Y still chooses the A/B relationship depth.

L07Locked

Bake movement into one file

Complete the A→B Lerp idea.

Choose any Morph mode and any authored movement you like. Keep A→B Lerp on.

Press BAKE.

Listen to the idea: the movement is no longer something your hand has to perform. It lives inside the baked IR.

CHAPTER 6

Baking is not the end

A baked IR can immediately become material for the next one.

R01Locked

Make Generation 2

Use your own result as new source material.

  1. Load one of your baked IRs back into A.
  2. Put something different in B.
  3. Choose another Morph mode or another curve.
  4. Bake again.

There is no rule saying Bake has to be the end. Repeated baking can build dense and complex soundfields from very simple starting material.

FINALLocked

Make one IR you would actually use

No recipe. This one is yours.

  1. Choose your own A and B.
  2. Shape them for a reason.
  3. Choose a Morph relationship because you like what it does.
  4. Keep it static or turn on A→B Lerp and author movement.
  5. Use EQ / EDIT / OUT only where you need them.
  6. Bake the result and give it a name you will recognize later.

Success: you can explain what you wanted, what you changed, what you heard, and why you kept it.

WHY I BUILT IT

Make old sounds become something new

I built Impulse Anvil because I was sick of downloading more sounds and getting more plugins that kept leading me back to the same places. I wanted something that could take old dusty sounds I already had and turn them into something new and interesting.

I was looking for dense, complex sound that was easy to apply without building a chain of different plugins. That's what eventually led me to IRs and Impulse Anvil.

I now use it on almost every track in some way. It doesn't always have to take over the sound. Sometimes everything in a track is already clean and on point, but something is missing behind it. A subtle Anvil texture can add depth and complexity while the main beat stays simple.

You can also build reusable material for specific jobs — even your own vocal IRs, or a different set for each artist you work with.
