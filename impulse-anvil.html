Real material creates
unrepeatable complexity.

Water, stone, metal, paper, rooms, machinery, footsteps, environmental noise and small accidental sounds can all become creative source material.

Found-sound convolution

A recording does not need to resemble a traditional reverb.

Short sounds can create tight tonal coloration and resonance. Longer recordings can introduce evolving spaces, irregular tails and rhythmic behavior that changes how a loop or performance is perceived.

Because the material begins in the real world, the result can carry irregularity and complexity that would be difficult to design deliberately with ordinary synthesis or modulation.

Choose the relationship,
not just the amount.

The full workstation contains 21 Morph relationships. You do not need to learn all of them first. Start with the question you are trying to answer.

Core Morph · Residual / Decompose · Acoustic Interaction

Sometimes you want a hybrid. Sometimes you want the difference.

The mode browser keeps the full library organized and explains what each relationship is useful for, how Morph behaves and what is happening underneath.

  • Core Morph: direct, spectral and stereo relationships.
  • Residual / Decompose: shared material, unique material and different kinds of leftovers.
  • Acoustic Interaction: compound, transfer, magnitude/phase and more experimental structural relationships.
  • Draw, Path, Glue and Omni can control depth through the selected relationship where supported by the mode.

Draw movement into the IR

Paint it. Route it. Glue it.
Make the gesture become time.

The curve display is now a construction surface. Draw controls the relationship, Path routes source-time, Glue assembles pieces, and Omni Path makes the full 2-D gesture contribute to output time.

Draw

Paint how A and B change

Draw the A/B or relationship movement directly through the IR timeline.

Path

Move through source-time

Horizontal travel is the clock. Move right to read later source material or left to revisit earlier material. Vertical movement changes A/B or relationship depth.

Glue Path

Assemble a new timeline from pieces

Each stroke becomes the next piece of the output. Glue keeps its own horizontal route timing and remains independent of Lerp Start and Lerp Time.

Omni Path

Make the whole gesture become time

X still reads the selected Lerp source window and Y still controls A/B or relationship depth. Omni changes the clock: both horizontal and vertical travel create output duration.

Omni timing in one sentence: Lerp Start offsets the source window Omni reads, Lerp Time scales the width of that window, and the drawn 2-D travel distance is converted through that window into the final IR timeline.

Honest fit

Know what you are buying.

Impulse Anvil is built for deliberate sound transformation through custom impulse-response construction, exploration and WAV creation rather than continuous performance automation of every shaping control.

It is a strong fit when you want to...

  • Reshape a sound already in your track instead of immediately replacing it
  • Build new IRs from two pieces of acoustic or recorded material
  • Choose structural relationships instead of relying on one normal crossfade
  • Draw movement, revisit source-time or assemble a response with Glue Path
  • Bake portable WAV IRs and build a growing personal library

Important before you buy

  • Many IR-shaping edits rebuild the response and are intended for design rather than continuous live knob automation
  • Heavy analysis modes and very long or dense material can require more preparation time
  • Windows 10/11, 64-bit VST3 is the primary supported commercial release.
  • macOS AU/VST3 is available as a testing build with full plug-in functionality. It is not distributed with an Apple Developer ID signature and is not notarized by Apple, so manual Gatekeeper approval may be required.
  • Delivery is a digital ZIP bundle rather than an installer; no physical item is shipped
  • Use source recordings and IRs you own or are permitted to process

FAQ

Clear answers before checkout.

What is Impulse Anvil?

Impulse Anvil is a sound-design workstation built around designing the impulse response itself. Put it on a playing instrument or audio track, build a response from two IRs, recordings or found sounds, and Anvil's own stereo convolution engine processes the track through that response while you design it. Bake is optional export.

Is it a convolution reverb?

It includes its own stereo convolution engine and processes your track through the response you are designing. The difference is that Anvil is centered on making and shaping that response instead of only choosing a finished reverb preset.

Do I need another IR loader?

ANVIL IS THE EFFECT. You do not need another convolution plugin to use the response you are designing. Anvil already processes the playing track through it. Bake only when you want the response itself as a reusable WAV for another compatible convolution tool or another Anvil design pass.

What is the difference between a preset and Bake?

A preset saves the complete Anvil setup so you can return to the tweaking session or keep a favorite configuration. Use the preset Save / Load controls in OPTIONS. Bake exports the designed response itself as a WAV.

Do A and B only crossfade?

No. The full version contains 21 relationships across Core Morph, Residual / Decompose and Acoustic Interaction families. They include direct and spectral blends, stereo structures, shared/unique material, residual relationships and more experimental transformations.

What are Draw, Path, Glue and Omni Path?

Draw paints how the selected relationship changes over the IR. Path adds forward/backward source-time travel. Glue Path appends separate painted pieces and owns its duration independently of Lerp Start/Time. Omni Path keeps X as source-time and Y as relationship depth, while both horizontal and vertical route travel create output time.

What does A→B Lerp do?

It creates one prepared IR whose relationship changes internally over its duration. The selected Morph mode and curve/path define the construction, and the result can be baked into one WAV instead of requiring live automation.

Can I use only what B has that A does not?

Unique B is designed around that exact relationship: progressively remove the component estimated to be shared with A and keep what remains characteristic of B. Matched Residual, Spectral Carve and Transfer provide related alternatives depending on what you are trying to isolate.

Can I load normal recordings and found sounds?

Yes. Compatible recordings can become convolution material. Short fragments often behave like complex resonant or tonal fingerprints; longer material can introduce more obvious timing and decay behavior.

What gets baked?

The prepared IR path is baked: A/B preparation, the current Morph/Lerp construction, Edit, IR-input preparation, EQ, Width, Normalize and Limiter as documented. Playback-only controls such as Dry/Wet and final track Output are not a substitute for preparing the IR itself.

Can I use baked WAVs elsewhere?

Yes. The full version writes standard WAV impulse responses for compatible convolution software and IR loaders.

What does the demo include?

The demo lets you test the Windows VST3 with the included library, Time Morph and a reduced Color workflow. It adds subtle periodic demo noise and does not unlock the complete Morph library, A→B Lerp or WAV baking.

What platforms are supported?

Windows 10/11, 64-bit VST3 is the primary supported commercial release. A macOS AU/VST3 testing build is also available from the Downloads page. The macOS testing build uses the same demo/full-license system and unlocks the complete plug-in functionality with a valid Impulse Anvil license, but it is not distributed with an Apple Developer ID signature and is not notarized by Apple, so macOS may require manual approval.

Is the macOS testing build feature-limited?

No. Testing-build status describes the current macOS distribution/support path, not a reduced feature tier. The build starts in the normal Impulse Anvil demo state; the same purchased license unlocks the complete functionality. The important difference is distribution trust: the macOS testing build is not Apple Developer ID signed and has not been notarized by Apple.
