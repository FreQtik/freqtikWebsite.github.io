The layers of matter

PHYSICAL HIERARCHY

“Chemistry” is one layer in a stack. Higher layers emerge from lower-level physics, but each layer has its own useful concepts.

1 · Quantum / electronsOrbitals, charge, electron energy levels, electromagnetic interaction.
2 · ChemicalBonds, ions, molecules, redox, acids/bases, reactions.
3 · Material / phaseSolids, liquids, gases, crystals, metals, interfaces, collective behavior.
4 · ThermodynamicEnergy, entropy, equilibrium, temperature, pressure, chemical potential.
5 · NuclearProtons/neutrons, isotopes, radioactivity, fusion, fission, transmutation.
6 · GravitationalMass-energy curves spacetime. Usually negligible at atomic/lab scales.
electronic structure → chemistry → material properties ↔ thermodynamics & kinetics    |    nuclei set element identity    |    mass-energy sources gravity
Like you're 12: The nucleus decides what element an atom is. The electrons decide most of how it connects to other atoms. Huge numbers of those connections create materials. Heat and pressure push those materials into different states. Nuclear changes are a deeper layer, and gravity mostly cares about total mass-energy.

Your core mental model

Ordinary chemistry rearranges electrons and atomic connections while the nuclei keep their element identities.

Changing bonds can radically change properties without changing any element. Carbon can participate in graphite, diamond, methane, carbon dioxide, polymers, proteins and countless other structures because structure matters.

Changing the number of protons is different: that changes the element and belongs to nuclear physics.

Energy & information

Energy is a measurable property of a physical system, not a fluid hidden inside atoms. Structure constrains what states and transformations are available. In the broad physics sense, a molecule therefore carries physical information in its state and structure, but chemical reactions are governed by quantum mechanics, electromagnetism, statistical mechanics, thermodynamics and kinetics.

State + physical laws + environment → allowed evolution of the system

Professional model

Two element names are never enough to uniquely determine a chemical product. Stoichiometry, oxidation state, molecular geometry, phase, solvent, temperature, pressure, electrochemical potential, surfaces and kinetics can all change the outcome.

identity → valence/electronic structure → possible bonding → thermodynamics → kinetics → actual products

Atoms do not snap together like LEGO with one fixed answer. Their outer electrons decide what kinds of connections are possible. Then the surroundings decide which connection actually happens and whether it happens fast enough to notice.

Reaction anatomy

What to look for in every chemical equation

AtomsThe number of nuclei of each element is conserved in ordinary chemistry.
Electrons / chargeElectron density or explicit electrons/ions can move, but total electric charge is conserved.
Bonds / structureOld interactions are broken or weakened and new interactions form. This is where chemical identity and properties can change dramatically.
EnergyEnergy is redistributed among bonds, molecular motion, radiation, surroundings and other degrees of freedom.
RateThe equation alone does not tell you whether the process is fast, slow, surface-limited, diffusion-limited, catalyzed or effectively frozen.

Thermodynamics: is a change favored? EXACT RELATION

At constant temperature and pressure, Gibbs free energy helps determine the thermodynamic direction of change.

ΔG = ΔH − TΔS

Kinetics: how fast can it happen? ARRHENIUS MODEL

A process can be thermodynamically favorable and still be extremely slow. The Arrhenius equation shows why temperature can strongly change a rate constant.

k ∝ exp(−Eₐ / RT)
Critical distinction: thermodynamics answers “is the final state lower in free energy under these conditions?” Kinetics answers “is there an accessible pathway, and how quickly is it crossed?” A catalyst changes the pathway/barrier; it does not change ΔG between the same initial and final states.

Energy scales: why “stronger chemistry” is the wrong picture

ThermalkBT ≈ 0.026 eV near room temperature
Chemical bondstypically order 1–10 eV per bond/particle-scale event
Atomic ionizationeV to tens of eV for many first ionizations
Nuclearoften keV–MeV per nuclear event
Nucleon rest energyorder 1 GeV per nucleon
Stars: gravity provides confinement and enormous pressure/density; high temperature provides a distribution of kinetic energies; quantum tunneling is essential. “Pressure alone makes elements” is therefore incomplete.

How much mass for Earth-like gravity?

At a distance of 1 meter from a compact mass, achieving 9.81 m/s² in Newtonian gravity would require about:

1.47 × 10¹¹ kg

That is roughly one hundred billion kilograms concentrated inside the 1 m radius. The problem is not finding a clever chemical fuel; it is the enormous mass-energy scale required.

Does chemistry affect gravity?

Yes in principle, but almost immeasurably in ordinary situations. Binding energy contributes to total mass via E=mc², so different chemical states have minuscule mass differences. Those differences are far too small to act like a practical “gravity generator.” Nuclear binding changes mass-energy more strongly than chemistry, yet still does not bypass the basic gravitational mass-energy requirement.

Layer impact

Use this reasoning pattern on the real world

1. What objects/states exist?Atoms, ions, molecules, crystal lattice, plasma, nuclei, fields?
2. What layer is changing?Electronic, chemical, phase/material, thermodynamic, nuclear, gravitational?
3. What conservation laws apply?Charge, energy, momentum, angular momentum, particle/nuclear bookkeeping as relevant.
4. Is it favorable?Thermodynamics/equilibrium: compare free energies under the actual conditions.
5. Can it happen in time?Kinetics: barriers, pathways, transport, diffusion, collisions, catalysts, surfaces.
6. Is the model still valid?At extreme temperature, pressure, fields or nuclear energies, everyday chemistry models can cease to be the right description.

Accuracy rules used by the app

ExactAtomic number = proton count; ordinary chemistry conserves element identity; charge bookkeeping and basic physical equations where stated.
CuratedNames, symbols, broad uses, characteristic chemistry and selected common compound examples.
ApproximateAufbau electron filling, isotope-stability band estimates, simple phase equations and family-level combination predictions.
Unknown/limitedSuperheavy-element bulk properties. The app labels predictions rather than pretending atom-at-a-time measurements are ordinary bulk chemistry.

Important model limits

Nuclear lifetime is isotope-specific. You cannot derive an exact half-life from proton/neutron counts using a simple slider model. Matter Lab therefore explains broad stability drivers and refuses to invent a precise lifetime.

“Can combine with” is not the same as “will react when mixed.” The app presents common compound families and periodic trends, then separates thermodynamic possibility from kinetic accessibility.

Hazardous chemistry remains conceptual. High-level outcomes can be educational; preparation instructions, quantities and operating conditions for dangerous substances are intentionally omitted.

Matter Lab Reality v0.3 is an educational simulator, not a laboratory procedure generator. All 118 current IUPAC elements are included; approximations and uncertain superheavy chemistry are labeled.
